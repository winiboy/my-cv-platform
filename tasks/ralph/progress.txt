Decision:
- **Lines, not height.** The shared computation takes line boxes, so it can reproduce how Chromium moves a whole line, and orphans and widows, to the next page. A height-only formula put the second marker of a 3-page letter 7 px low.

- **Whole-px page edges.** The page constants come from Chromium's printed page (1123 px, margins rounded to whole px), not from mm, after the PDF-anchored test showed the mm values put markers one line early.

Decision:
- **Measure, don't estimate.** The server lays the letter out in the PDF renderer's own browser and fonts, and pages it with the same function as the preview. A word or character estimate would have made "the PDF is the authority" untrue.
- **When in doubt, keep the first letter.** No slot, a timeout, a failure, an unusable reply or too little time all keep it. Creation and generation never fail because of the one-page check.
- **German sets the budget.** 230 words with 2 body paragraphs is the largest that fits for German; the other languages have more room.

Open owner decisions:
- **CI coverage:** CI's integration job has no Chromium, so the measure-vs-PDF test skips there. CI's e2e runs on a production build and covers the real measurement on the creation path. Agreement on the serverless runtime (`@sparticuz/chromium` with the substitute fonts) is not tested before deployment.
- **Render slots:** a measurement holds one of the 2 per-instance PDF slots for about 0.2 s, at most 10 s. A PDF download on the same warm instance at that moment can get a 503 with `Retry-After: 5`.
- **Longer creations:** a letter that runs over now takes a second AI call, a few seconds more.
